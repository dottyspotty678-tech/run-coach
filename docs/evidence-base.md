# Evidence base for a running training and nutrition agent

A curated set of primary scientific documents — consensus statements, position stands and peer-reviewed syntheses — to ground an AI agent giving running training and nutrition advice. All are the references sports dietitians and endurance coaches cite in practice. Most are open access via PMC.

Compiled 23 Aug 2026.

## Nutrition

**1. Nutrition and Athletic Performance — Academy of Nutrition and Dietetics / Dietitians of Canada / ACSM joint position statement (2016)**
The foundational, evidence-graded reference covering energy needs, macronutrients, fluids, supplements and periodised nutrition across training and competition. Treat as a core reference document.
https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/26891166/

**2. ISSN position stand: Nutrient timing (Kerksick et al., 2017)**
Quantified guidance on carbohydrate and protein timing, including 8–12 g carbohydrate/kg/day for glycogen maximisation and 1.2 g/kg/h refeeding when recovery time is under 4 hours.
https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC5596471/

**3. A step towards personalized sports nutrition: Carbohydrate intake during exercise (Jeukendrup, 2014)**
Duration-dependent in-race fuelling framework: single carbohydrate sources oxidise at up to ~60 g/h; ~90 g/h of multiple transportable carbohydrates (glucose + fructose) for ultra-endurance events, independent of body weight.
https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC4008807/

**4. IOC consensus statement: Dietary supplements and the high-performance athlete (Maughan et al., 2018)**
Evidence review finding only a few supplements — caffeine, creatine, buffering agents and nitrate — have good evidence of performance benefit, plus risk management guidance.
https://stillmed.olympics.com/media/Documents/Athletes/Medical-Scientific/Consensus-Statements/2018_dietary-supplements-high-performance-athlete.pdf

**5. 2023 IOC consensus statement on Relative Energy Deficiency in Sport (REDs) (Mountjoy et al., 2023)**
Covers low energy availability, its health and performance consequences, and a validated clinical assessment tool. Include as the safety layer: it stops an agent recommending aggressive calorie deficits to high-mileage runners.
British Journal of Sports Medicine 2023;57:1073–1097. https://bjsm.bmj.com/content/57/17/1073

**6. ACSM position stand: Exercise and fluid replacement (Sawka et al., 2007)**
Hydration before, during and after exercise: prevent >2% body-weight loss during exercise and customise fluid plans to individual sweat rates.
https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/17277604/

## Running training

**7. The training characteristics of world-class distance runners (Haugen et al., 2022)**
The single best training-side document: integrates scientific literature and results-proven elite practice covering weekly volumes, session counts, periodisation and the finding that ≥80% of running volume is performed at low intensity year-round. Treat as a core reference document.
https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC8975965/

**8. The training intensity distribution among well-trained and elite endurance athletes (Stöggl & Sperlich, 2015)**
Defines polarized (~80:20 low:high), pyramidal and threshold models and reviews the evidence for each.
https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC4621419/

**9. Polarized vs other training intensity distributions: systematic review with meta-analysis (Oliveira et al., 2024)**
The most current meta-analytic comparison of intensity distribution approaches for endurance performance.
https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC11329428/

**10. Does polarized training improve performance in recreational runners? (Muñoz, Seiler et al., 2014)**
Randomised trial of 30 recreational runners across polarized vs between-thresholds distributions over 10 weeks against 10K performance. Directly relevant if the agent serves everyday athletes rather than elites.
https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/23752040/

**11. Strength training and running economy: systematic review with meta-analysis (Llanos-Lagos et al., 2024)**
High-load and combined strength methods produce small-to-moderate improvements in running economy; plyometrics help at lower speeds. Builds on Blagrove et al. (2018), which recommends two to three strength sessions per week using varied modalities.
https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC11052887/

**12. Crossing the golden training divide / Haugen middle-distance companion review (2021–2022)**
Companion coverage of 800–1500 m training if the agent needs middle-distance scope.
https://link.springer.com/article/10.1186/s40798-022-00438-7

## Notes for the agent build

- Ingest full papers rather than abstracts where licensing allows; the PMC and Sports Medicine – Open links give full text.
- Treat items 1 and 7 as core reference documents, the rest as supporting knowledge.
- Known gap: running injury prevention and load progression. The evidence there (e.g. acute:chronic workload ratio) is more contested than the nutrition and intensity-distribution literature, so it needs more careful framing.
- Verify each URL resolves to full text before ingestion; some publisher pages are abstract-only.
